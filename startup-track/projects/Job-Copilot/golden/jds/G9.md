# Senior Product Manager — Core Product
**Company:** Alderpoint (B2B SaaS) · **Location:** Remote (global; async-first) · **Team:** Core Product

## About the role
Alderpoint is hiring a Senior PM for our core product. We have an opinionated product culture, and we describe it in specific language because the words carry meaning for us. We run a **continuous discovery habit** with weekly customer touchpoints. We use an **opportunity solution tree** to connect an **outcome over output** mindset to a single **north-star metric**. We think in **jobs to be done**, practise **dual-track agile**, and start every bet with **assumption mapping** and a **riskiest assumption test**. We work as a **product trio** (PM, design, eng), use **story mapping** to slice releases, and hold a **narrative-driven roadmap** instead of a feature list. We make **one-way vs two-way door** decisions deliberately, run a **pre-mortem** before big bets, tie our **definition of done to an outcome**, and protect a **guardrail metric** on every experiment. Above all we are **customer-problem-first**.

## What you'll do
- Own a core product area against a north-star metric, using the opportunity solution tree to choose what to build.
- Run the continuous discovery habit; bring evidence from weekly customer touchpoints to every prioritisation call.
- Lead your product trio through dual-track agile: discovery and delivery in parallel.
- Frame bets with assumption mapping and test the riskiest assumption first.

## Requirements (must have)
- 6+ years in product with evidence of owning an outcome against a north-star metric; **required**.
- Real discovery practice — not vocabulary, but decisions you changed because of customer evidence; **required**.
- Track record slicing and shipping through a delivery team; **required**.

## Nice to have
- B2B SaaS experience.
- Familiarity with our specific practices above.

## How we work
We use this language every day, so a lot of applicants send it right back to us. That doesn't move us. Anyone can write "continuous discovery" and "outcome over output" on a resume; we're reading for the *decision* behind the phrase — the feature you killed, the assumption that proved false, the outcome the north-star actually moved. Vocabulary is free. Evidence isn't.

# Founding Product Manager — Seed-Stage Startup
**Company:** Driftwood (seed-stage, ~14 people) · **Location:** Remote (global) · **Team:** you are the product team

## About the role
Driftwood is a seed-stage startup building a workflow tool for independent creative studios. We have a rough product, early customers, and no PM yet. You would be our founding PM: the whole product function, wearing every hat, from customer discovery to writing acceptance criteria to jumping on a support call. This is a 0→1 role. Scope is enormous but shallow — you will do a bit of everything, own outcomes directly, and live with real resource constraints. It is the opposite of owning one polished slice of a mature product inside a large org.

## What you'll do
- Own the entire product: discovery, prioritisation, delivery, and outcomes, with no PM peers and minimal process.
- Talk to customers constantly and turn raw signal into the next two weeks of work.
- Ship scrappily and measure honestly; find the wedge that gets us from early traction to a repeatable motion.
- Wear the hats around product: light design, light data, support triage, even sales-engineering when needed.
- Set just enough process to move fast without breaking the few things customers rely on.

## Requirements (must have)
- 4+ years in product, with **direct 0→1 or early-stage evidence** — you built something from little, not just optimised something mature; **required**.
- Demonstrated breadth: owning outcomes across discovery, delivery, and go-to-market with scarce resources; **required**.
- Comfort with ambiguity and near-zero process; **required**.
- Evidence you shipped and moved a real outcome, not just managed a roadmap; **required**.

## Nice to have
- Prior startup or founding experience.
- Experience selling or supporting your own product.

## How we work
We are logo-blind by necessity. A big-name employer on your resume tells us nothing about whether you can build from nothing with 14 people and no safety net — some of the best big-tech PMs would drown here, and some unknown-startup PMs would thrive. We read for scope and evidence, not brand. Show us what you built when no one handed you a spec.
